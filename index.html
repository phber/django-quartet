The Band

Le Style Manouche

In a corner booth of a dimly lit room, four musicians lean into a single microphone the way their forebears did outside the Hôtel Claridge in 1934 — no drums, no amplifiers, just wood, wire, and breath. The Django Quartet plays acoustic Gypsy Jazz in the lineage of the Quintette du Hot Club de France, built around the same instrumentation Django Reinhardt and Stéphane Grappelli made legendary: lead guitar, rhythm guitars driving la pompe, violin, and upright bass.

We chase that same restless, cigarette-smoke energy — the breakneck runs, the aching minor-key ballads, the joy of a room that swings without a single drumbeat. Every arrangement is built by ear and by hand, the way it was before amplifiers made it easy to be loud instead of alive.

Whether we're opening for a wine bar's Tuesday crowd or closing out a private soirée until the candles gutter, the Quartet brings the sound of a Montmartre cellar club straight to the room — acoustic, intimate, and unmistakably hot.
